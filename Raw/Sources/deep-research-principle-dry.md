## Normalized Content

DRY — “Don't Repeat Yourself” — was formulated by Andy Hunt and Dave Thomas in the 1999 edition of The Pragmatic Programmer. Its canonical statement: “Every piece of knowledge must have a single, unambiguous, authoritative representation within a system.” The publisher reproduces this wording in its Pragmatic Programmer Tips collection, and in the Artima interview on orthogonality Dave Thomas stressed that DRY is much broader than eliminating repeated source-code fragments: it applies to business rules, schemas, configuration, build procedures, tests, scripts, and documentation. The operative distinction is duplication of knowledge versus merely similar code. Canonical anchors include pragprog.com, the Artima interview, GitLab/GitHub reuse documentation, OpenGitOps, Kustomize/Helm single-source mechanisms, and the laws of software engineering summary.
