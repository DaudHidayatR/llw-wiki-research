## Normalized Content

The KISS principle is normally expressed as “Keep it simple, stupid” and is commonly attributed to Clarence “Kelly” Johnson, the Lockheed engineer who led the organization later known as the Skunk Works. Two historical propositions must be kept separate: (1) Johnson promoted radical practical simplicity in aircraft design and project organization — strongly consistent with his documented methods, the Skunk Works operating rules, and aircraft such as the U-2; (2) the precise claim that the word or acronym “KISS” first appeared in a specific 1954 U-2 design report requires the primary document itself, which the research record did not retrieve. Canonical anchors include Lockheed Martin's own publications on Johnson's 14 Rules and the U-2, Saltzer's end-to-end arguments, RFC 3439's simplicity recommendations, Dijkstra's EWD340, NIST SSDF, and POSIX/Shell standards for simple, composable shell interfaces.
