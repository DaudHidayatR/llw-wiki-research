## Normalized Content

Tell, Don't Ask is a design heuristic: when an object or module owns state and the rules governing that state, ask it to perform the relevant operation rather than extracting its data, making the decision elsewhere, and pushing a result back — the account.withdraw(amount) form instead of reading balance, deciding, and writing it back. The principle is best understood as advice about responsibility and decision ownership, not as a ban on getters, return values, predicates, or queries. Its documented history is more nuanced than the common claim that Alec Sharp coined the name in 1996: Sharp articulated the underlying idea in his Smalltalk writing, Allen Holub's JavaWorld articles (1998) and The Pragmatic Programmer popularized it, and Martin Fowler's bliki fixed the modern phrasing. Canonical anchors include the Smalltalk by Example text, Holub's articles (holub.com, InfoWorld), Fowler's bliki, and the original Pragmatic Programmer material.
