## Normalized Content

SOLID is a mnemonic for five principles of object-oriented design: Single Responsibility, Open-Closed, Liskov Substitution, Interface Segregation, and Dependency Inversion. Robert C. Martin's 2000 paper “Design Principles and Design Patterns” is an important consolidation but not the original source of all five: Bertrand Meyer formulated the Open-Closed Principle; Barbara Liskov's work on data abstraction and behavioral subtyping underlies LSP (Liskov & Wing 1994); Martin developed or popularized SRP, ISP, DIP, and the object-oriented formulation of LSP. The SOLID acronym came later and is generally credited to Michael Feathers. For shell/infrastructure code the transferable cores are SRP (one reason to change per module), DIP (depend on stable interfaces such as CLI contracts), and information hiding; canonical anchors include the archived objectmentor paper, Uncle Bob's clarifications, and the Google Shell Style Guide.
